Carden Taylor

I am a sophomore at the Colorado School of Mines, majoring in Geophysical Engineering with a focus on climate geophysics and a minor in Applied Mathematics and Statistics. My work centers on translating physical Earth-system processes into quantitative models, numerical approximations, and statistical comparisons against observational records.

Outside of coursework and numerical modeling, I enjoy spending time in the mountains around Golden and building small analytical and coding projects. I am open to undergraduate research opportunities, summer internships, and collaborative scientific work.

Ongoing Work

November 2026: Founded Climate@Mines, a student-organization centered on understanding climate science and promoting climate awareness. The first official meeting will occur in December, 2026. Current goals of the student organization include bringing in climate scientists to discuss their research, organizing student outreach events on campus to promote climate literacy, and the development of an on-campus weather station.

October 2026: Completed the transient zero-dimensional energy-balance climate model (0D EBM (1900–2025)) incorporating IPCC / Myhre et al. (1998) radiative forcing for CO2, CH4, and N2O, integrated via Forward Euler's method and benchmarked against NASA GISTEMP v4 (RMSE = 0.2369 °C, R2 ≈ 0.528).
Research Interests: Radiative forcing and climate sensitivity, ocean mixed-layer heat uptake and transient dynamics, numerical ODE integration, and geophysical time-series validation.
